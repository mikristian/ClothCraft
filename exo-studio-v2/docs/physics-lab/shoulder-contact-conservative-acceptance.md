# Conservative surface-contact acceptance experiment — reverted

Tested an optional `continuousSurfaceContact` guard in `correctAvatarSurfaceIntrusion` that sampled **actual** structural constraint strains (peak and P95) and sewn/virtual seam gaps (peak and P95) before and after candidate corrections. The guard accepted only a strict decrease in sampled penetrating faces, a maximum-intrusion increase of at most the existing 0.5 mm allowance, and no increase in any of the four physical metrics except `128 × Number.EPSILON × max(1,|baseline|)` roundoff. On rejection it restored positions, `previous` Verlet state and `contactDepth`. Proposal metrics and reasons were exposed in a diagnostic result.

The focused standard tee regression proved that the guard rejected a proposal reducing penetration while raising P95 strain 1.71657 → 1.73539, maximum seam gap 52.212 → 53.881 mm and P95 gap 30.274 → 32.346 mm. The regression checked exact restoration of the particle position, Verlet history and `contactDepth`. All checks passed.

However, the full **48-case / 120-frame** matrix revealed serious global regressions relative to the previous per-frame contact acceptance. On the standard 180 cm tee with midpoint stabilization enabled and triangle assist disabled, strict guarded continuous contact raised peak strain **206.90% → 585.45%**, P95 **23.69% → 37.28%**, maximum seam gap **33.88 → 34.91 mm**, and penetrating faces **7 → 20**. On modified 180 cm with midpoint stabilization and triangle assist enabled, peak strain **203.60% → 462.45%**, P95 **27.87% → 35.27%**, maximum seam gap **34.25 → 36.05 mm**, and penetrating faces **6 → 8**. Other fixture outcomes varied: some improved, others worsened. The guard reduced acceptance frequency but prevented correction history that was beneficial to eventual solver convergence.

**Decision: reverted the guard and its now-inapplicable regression script, retained this research note.** The original `correctAvatarSurfaceIntrusion` implementation and all existing opt-in flags remain as before this experiment. Stronger *local* inequalities alone do not guarantee a better 120-frame garment fit because nonlinear seam/contact dynamics depend on prior frame positions and velocities. Future work should use a shared strain/sewing/contact solve, compare complete trajectories, and validate several garment/avatar cases before changing the optional correction acceptance policy. No avatar-height gates or fixture-tuned threshold were introduced. No production app release.
