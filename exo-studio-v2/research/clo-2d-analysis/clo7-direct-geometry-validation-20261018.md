### Why earlier first clicks appeared to do nothing

The bottom hem probe clicked around `(815,543)`, close to multiple overlapping horizontal hem/internal/cut lines; the before/after screenshot was unchanged. A later click on a clearly visible outer side seam at `(795,470)` **did** insert a point. The inspector also added a genuine `mouseMoved` event followed by a short hover and mouse-down interval; the earlier instant click path may have collapsed for CLO's custom viewport. The failed hem click should not be taken as evidence that the Add Point tool is broken.

### Behavior that still needs a controlled test

1. **Internal seam-reference persistence.** The colored sewing marks stayed in place, but the live CLO UI did not expose stable sewing segment IDs. A later DXF-AAMA/ASTM export or an explicit Sewing Edit visualization before and after a split is needed. CLO's saved native ZPAC/PAC binary is proprietary and cannot be used as an invented JSON seam schema.
2. **Exact type of free curve point.** `Edit Curve Point` produced editable highlighted points distinct from the ordinary point-insertion mode; proving whether CLO internally adds a curve-shaping node versus a true contour vertex requires a direct curve point delete/split comparison or exported controlled shape, and must not be guessed from a colored circle.
3. **Redo isolation.** The visual Redo/Undo sequence was captured, but simultaneous viewport scaling makes an absolute shape-alignment assertion weaker than the demonstrated pixel-exact Undo from a controlled curve drag.
4. **Self-intersection warning cause.** The displayed Information text was real. Do not describe the precise violated seam/contour or geometric cause without an isolated and reproducible input. Add preflight intersection validation in EXO on point/curve/split transactions, and retain a safe user-facing rejection reason.
5. **Curvature, numeric splits, POM.** Direct `Edit Curvature` segment pull, exact split ratio, graded persistent Points of Measure, and mapped curve-points after Save/Reload remain future tests.

## Engineering consequences for EXO ClothCraft V2

The native Rust polygon schema `research/oss/clothcraft/src/garment/polygon.rs` already has stable named `edges`, physical-metre points and cubic handles, seam allowances, notches and target lengths. However `Command::CurvePoint` in `research/oss/clothcraft/src/authoring.rs` simply toggles cubic handles on the incoming/outgoing edges of a topological vertex. CLO's separate `Edit Curve Point` interaction is directly observed as a distinct mode with independent highlighted shaping points, requiring its own first-class selection/edit path instead of reusing the existing command's name uncritically.

The native `SplitBoundary` currently refuses to split a seam-connected edge or one with target lengths, notches or allowances. CLO visibly inserted a point on a torso side with sewing indicators, but the **seam-attachment migration cannot yet be certified by screenshot**. Build a unit-tested stable-ID split/remap transaction before loosening Rust's safe refusal. Validate both full-segment and fractional free-sewing records, notches and target length references, symmetric paired edges, seam allowance continuity, and Undo/Redo.

The tested selected curve length has separately displayed 2D and 3D values in CLO, and geometry changes update the 2D readout immediately. EXO should keep planar 2D cutline arc length, simulated 3D seam length and symmetric totals separate, and ensure selected line/curve controls recalculate the measured length as they move. The direct tests also show that Undo must be a full geometry transaction, with original contour recovered, rather than merely clearing selection.
